About

I hold a B.Sc. in Agriculture from the International University of Business Agriculture and Technology (IUBAT) in Dhaka, Bangladesh. Throughout my undergraduate studies, I was actively engaged in both computational and laboratory research.

At IUBAT, my work focused on computational plant biophysics, studying how stress-responsive proteins form biomolecular condensates under heat, drought, and salinity in crops like rice and soybean. Later, as a research intern at Sher-e-Bangla Agricultural University (SAU), I evaluated multi-generational tomato breeding lines, analyzing fruit quality, postharvest shelf life, and trait trade-offs.

Research Vision

I plan to investigate how plants sense abiotic stress, translate those signals inside the cell, and take protective action. I am also keen to explore plant-microbe and plant-environment interactions, aiming to understand how these dynamics can be harnessed to develop climate-resilient crops.

Research Interests

Abiotic Stress Sensing & Signaling

How plants perceive temperature extremes, drought, and salinity and translate signals at the cellular level.

Biomolecular Condensates (LLPS)

Phase separation of stress-responsive RNA-binding proteins into stress granules during environmental shock.

Plant-Microbe & Environment

Rhizosphere and phyllosphere interactions that modulate stress tolerance and plant adaptation.

Crop Breeding & Postharvest Quality

Multi-generational trait selection, biochemical quality retention, and ambient storage durability.
